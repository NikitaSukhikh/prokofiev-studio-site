03 / About

A living cultural organism.

Prokofiev Studio is a creative space in the heart of Hackney, dedicated to preserving and reimagining the artistic legacy of the Prokofiev family.

Rooted in a remarkable archive while embracing contemporary practice, the studio serves as a place for visual art, music, performance and interdisciplinary dialogue.

At its core is an important collection of works by artist Oleg Prokofiev, whose paintings, sculptures and works on paper form the foundation of the programme. Alongside this visual heritage is an active musical environment shaped by composer Gabriel Prokofiev.

Historical archives inspire new ideas as artists, musicians, writers and audiences come together through exhibitions, concerts, talks, workshops and experimental projects.

The programme is curated by Anzhela Popova, bringing archival research, contemporary practice and international cultural dialogue into conversation.

04 / Legacy

Past, in motion.

The studio holds a significant collection of Oleg Prokofiev’s paintings, sculptures, drawings and previously unseen archival material.

For fifteen years, this body of work remained stored in a barn in Norfolk, hidden from public view. It has now been relocated to London. Legacy here is not only about preservation — it is about continuity, using history as a catalyst for new ideas.

The Prokofiev legacy continues through Gabriel Prokofiev, composer, producer and founder of the independent record label Nonclassical.

Solo exhibition by Oleg Prokofiev

Beyond the Line

July — September 2026 · Curated by Anzhela Popova

Beyond the Line explores a decisive moment in Oleg Prokofiev’s artistic evolution, when drawing, painting and sculpture began to dissolve into one another through an increasingly radical exploration of line, colour and space.

The exhibition focuses on work created after Prokofiev’s first visit to the United States in 1977–78. New York’s verticality, energy and architectural rhythm inspired the remarkable series of drawings titled Stripes, while encounters with abstract painting on an unprecedented scale encouraged a new confidence in reduction, colour and formal clarity.

After returning, Prokofiev developed a language in which colour became an active structural force. This search extended naturally into sculpture. Wood became his principal medium for investigating form in three dimensions: an orchestration of movement, balance and void where empty space matters as much as material.

The exhibition reveals the pivotal transition where line becomes structure, colour becomes space and sculpture becomes a living choreography of balance and motion.

Group exhibition during Hackney Art Week

Yield

12—20 June 2026 · Curated by Caroline McCambridge & Aileen Kelly

Aileen Kelly · Alex Heim · Ana Genovés · Aya Fukami · Belinda Worsley · Caroline McCambridge · Jasone Miranda-Bilbao · Martina O’Shea · Ornela Novello · Tessa Garland

Yield explores what it means to bend, adapt, accommodate or give way in response to the pressures around us. Through a range of materials and approaches, it reflects on how tension, resilience, fragility and change shape everyday life.

Rather than seeing yielding as weakness or surrender, the exhibition approaches it as a form of responsiveness — a way of remaining open and adaptable within changing conditions.

Oleg Prokofiev · Valentino Vannini · Kirill Basalaev

Bending Time

1—29 May 2026 · Curated by Anzhela Popova

Bending Time is structured around the archive — understood not as a static repository, but as a living, generative system through which artistic legacies are continuously reactivated and brought into the present.

At its core is a reconstruction of Oleg Prokofiev’s 1990s studio on Dace Road in Hackney Wick. His wooden sculptures are encountered as part of an ongoing process, developed through the principle he described as the “line in space”.

Works by Valentino Vannini and Kirill Basalaev expand the framework through shared concerns with materiality, temporality and transformation. The result is not a stable narrative but a shifting constellation where the past is reactivated and meaning remains in motion.

Solo exhibition by Oleg Prokofiev

Composing Space

13—27 February 2026 · Curated by Anzhela Popova

The exhibition traces how New York transformed Oleg Prokofiev’s artistic language in 1977. The city’s scale and verticality, together with encounters with Rothko, Still, Sol LeWitt, Motherwell, Newman and Frankenthaler, led to stronger colours and a new approach to painting and drawing.

This initiated a period of experimentation with wood, fragments of metal and found materials. Prokofiev called the result “line in space”: a tangible line free to move in any direction but always returning to its origin.

His drawings and sculptures follow a logic akin to musical composition, with rhythm emerging from the balance between order and controlled disruption.

Poetry evening

Oleg Prokofiev

8 July 2026 · Read by Gabriel Prokofiev & Maria Bregman

An evening dedicated to the poetry of Oleg Prokofiev, offering a rare opportunity to experience his literary voice alongside his artistic legacy.

Prokofiev left behind several hundred poems, all written in Russian and preserved within his family archive. His poetic language is imagistic and fragmentary, often resisting translation. Dispensing with punctuation, he relies on line breaks to shape meaning.

Documentary screening by Maria Bregman

I Choose to Live

23 April 2026 · Duration 49 min

Lydia Grigorieva. I Choose to Live opens in the poet’s London home and garden before travelling into memories of her childhood in the Far North. Grigorieva reads her poetry as archive footage evokes the 1960s “Thaw” and the writers around her.

Dedicated to the poet’s 80th anniversary, the film moves through personal loss while revealing an inner world where the earthly and eternal intertwine.
